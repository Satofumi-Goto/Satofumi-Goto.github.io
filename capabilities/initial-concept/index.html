02Service blueprint

STAGE EVIDENCE Plan: consent + receipt time   Request: eligible offer + capacity   Confirm: accepted terms + committed holds   Travel: observed pickup + owned exception   Complete: trip / activity actuals + cost

Exception loop

A revised plan releases obsolete holds only after reconciliation. Cancellation or capacity loss updates positioning, ETA and the recovery owner.

Traceability contract
intentId + passenger / shipmentId + bookingRev + routeRev + missionId

Record offers, accepted terms, original / revised times, route stops and segment occupancy. Link every passenger and shipment to observed boarding, alighting or delivery. Preserve source / receipt time, consent and custody evidence. Shared mission costs remain unique.

Acceptance invariants

Positioning uses information already received. Commit all required holds before confirmation. Check seats, accessible space, payload and cargo volume on every segment. Recompute existing passenger limits after insertion. Time shifts preserve request identity. Suggestions need acceptance. Unknown effects require reconciliation. Completion uses passenger / cargo evidence separately. Each exception retains an owner and deadline.

MVP boundary and product prioritization

A complete service slice provides the baseline. Five controlled mechanisms test information value and shared-capacity economics.

03MVP & prioritization

Priority rule

Rank by measured service benefit, net contribution, feasibility and learning value.

Common fleet / hubs / demand conditions support comparison. Capacity and service guardrails remain binding.

Dependency orderReliable request / outcome records precede effect measurement. Finite capacity and recovery precede offer expansion.

Pilot MVP

One selected service slice

  1. 01Capture intent, needs, consent and idempotent request
  2. 02Return feasible options, binding reasons and expiry
  3. 03Commit booking + resource bundle with revision checks
  4. 04Track state, freshness, support case and recovery owner
  5. 05Reconcile actual trip, service use and passenger outcome

Exit only when normal + recovery paths pass acceptance; operator can resolve exceptions; event and cost records reconcile.
Required evidence: valid, rejected, cancelled and recovered journeys. Reconcile every request to one final disposition and every cost to one owner.

Iterate

Five controlled mechanisms

  1. 01Use received plans for feasible advance positioning
  2. 02Offer activities within spare time and capacity
  3. 03Shift accepted trips with clear timing and price
  4. 04Pool compatible riders within each detour limit
  5. 05Insert eligible cargo within payload and time limits

Advance when gains survive service guardrails, realistic acceptance, full costs and supply stress.
Retain independent controls for all five effects. Test late signals, substitution, peak overload, no-shows, cargo dwell and failed handovers.

Extend platform

Repeatable operating model

  1. 01Versioned adapters and purpose-limited consent
  2. 02Energy, hub throughput and staffing in feasibility
  3. 03Guarded support and owned passenger recovery
  4. 04Calibrate by zone, time and user segment
  5. 05Reusable configuration, evidence and rollback

Expand after second-site tests preserve service quality and net contribution with local demand, capacity and operating costs. Replication tests: different OD pattern, provider capacity, support load and energy limits. Retain tenant isolation and configuration / policy versions.

MVP control

MVP entry criteria: selected geography / ODD / channel / segment; named service and incident owners; provider, fleet and site capacity confirmed; legal, privacy, accessibility and partner constraints reviewed; C01–C07 data and authority boundaries agreed. Pilot exit: normal + cancellation + disruption / recovery traces pass; queue and support coverage are staffed; KPI baseline, missing-data behavior and cost ledger reconcile; rollback and customer communication are rehearsed. Optional C05 optimization, remote assistance and I09 roadside sensing stay out until separately gated.

G2 constraint register: ODD = approved operating conditions (road, speed, weather, time, etc.). DDT = real-time operational / tactical functions needed to operate in traffic (including OEDR); record which actor performs them. Define DDT fallback separately by actor, trigger, response and intervention boundary. Also record operating limits (hours / staffing / recovery), infrastructure (node power / energy / footprint) and business limits (fare / demand / transit overlap). For each, record variable, unit / value or TBD, source URL, owner, affected KPI and PL line. Set thresholds only after evidence and ownership are confirmed. Stress tests

Sweep lead time, forecast error, acceptance, substitution and discounts. Vary route overlap, party size, allowed detour, cargo mass / volume, handling time and delivery windows. Test peak demand, hub loss, no-shows, absent recipients and vehicle recovery. Preserve passenger / shipment identity. Reject future-information leakage. Gains fail when realistic costs erase contribution or any accepted service limit breaks.

Proposed tests only. Simulation outputs do not establish field impact or guarantee service-level attainment.

Reproducible evidence record

Record engine / scenario version, input units / sources, demand trace, information receipt times, initial fleet / hub state, seed and effect switches. Retain offer / intent linkage, acceptance, original / revised time, trip / activity actuals, rejected demand and ledger entries. Report paired differences and uncertainty across repeated runs.
Field design: predefine assignment unit, observation window and thresholds. Shared vehicles create spillover across users and zones. Check non-participation, displacement and rebound demand. Keep simulated acceptance distinct from measured behavior.
Decision packet: input provenance, reproducible run, outcome distribution, guardrail failures, sensitivity, accountable owner and a documented continue / revise / stop decision. Unsupported effects stay unvalidated.

Operating feasibility and verification evidence

A configuration qualifies only within its tested demand, geography and operating conditions. Service quality, resource limits, recovery and economics require separate evidence.

21.1Measurement chain and explicit denominators

  1. RequestsUnique service intents
  2. DispatchVerified assignment
  3. PickupPhysical arrival
  4. CompletionVerified trip outcome

Service quality

  • Dispatch success = assigned / eligible dispatch requests.
  • Keep capacity and admission rejects in the denominator.
  • Demand fulfillment = completed / eligible requests.
  • Report customer cancellations separately by reason.

Pickup and utilization

Wait starts at request time for immediate demand, or agreed pickup time for a reservation. Report p50 / p95 with the observed-arrival count and missing outcomes. Show on-time pickups / pickups due, including failures. Separate available hours from scheduled fleet hours.

At cut-off, classify every passenger booking and shipment separately: completed, cancelled, rejected, expired, unresolved or active. A pooled mission is not one passenger request. Retain people counts separately from booking counts. Show exclusions, censoring and missing evidence.

PASS RECORD

21.3Capacity decision and business consequence

Feasible alternatives

Compare fleet, hub placement and service policy only after all required operating conditions pass. Rank cost within the feasible tested set. If none qualifies, report the binding constraint and re-plan.

  • Count shared route / infrastructure costs once.
  • Include dwell, displaced trips, handling and returns.
  • Separate operating contribution, CAPEX and cash flow.

Shared rides: route feasibility and passenger outcomes

04. Sharing can improve passenger output per vehicle-hour when compatible journeys fit the same route. Seat occupancy alone does not prove better service or contribution.

22.1Request compatibility and guarded route insertion

  1. Eligible requests
    • Pickup / arrival windows
    • Party size and accessible space
    • Sharing consent and fare terms
    • Maximum accepted ride time
  2. Candidate insertion
    • Insert pickup before drop-off
    • Recompute every downstream ETA
    • Check capacity on every segment
    • Keep existing passenger promises
  3. Commit and observe
    • Hold seats and route revision
    • Confirm accepted terms per party
    • Observe boarding / alighting
    • Keep each intent and outcome

Capacity along the route

For each segment s: Σ_i onboard(i,s) × partySize_i ≤ seats_usable(s)
  • Check accessible positions and luggage space separately under the vehicle configuration.
  • A pooled route is one vehicle mission with several passenger bookings. Count its distance once.

earliestPickup_i ≤ pickup_i ≤ latestPickup_i
arrival_i ≤ latestArrival_i
rideTime_i − directTime_i ≤ detourAllowance_i
  • Direct time uses the same traffic / time basis.
  • Include boarding, alighting and intermediate dwell.

Insertion and consent rules

  • Recheck every onboard and committed passenger.
  • Protect accepted windows and access requirements.
  • Offer sharing price and allowed detour explicitly.
  • An infeasible insertion returns a binding reason.
  • No-show or cancellation triggers reconciliation.

22.3Revenue gain, service loss and the comparison basis

  1. Passenger output
    • Passenger journeys / vehicle-hour
    • Passenger-km / vehicle-km
    • Seat-km used / available seat-km
    • Wait, detour and missed pickups
  2. Net contribution
    • Recognized fares after discounts
    • Less route, energy and support cost
    • Include added dwell and recovery
    • Route cost allocated only once
  3. Comparable alternatives
    • Same underlying passenger intents
    • Same fleet, hubs and faults
    • Single-party vs shared operation
    • Rejected and displaced trips visible

22.4Failure cases and release evidence

Stress cases

Mismatched OD / low overlap; full accessible space; delayed boarding; no-show after insertion; traffic delay; cancellation while onboard; vehicle loss with several parties needing recovery.

Stop adding riders when an accepted passenger limit or response-capacity constraint would fail.

Evidence: route revision, ordered stops, segment occupancy, accepted detour / fare, actual passenger times and booking-level outcomes. Show total contribution and passenger fulfillment together. Higher occupancy can coexist with fewer completed journeys. Agree thresholds and operating authority before pilot release.

05. Cargo uses compatible spare route and vehicle capacity. Incremental freight revenue must cover handling and detour costs while preserving passenger service commitments.

Independent constraints

Passenger seats and accessible positions remain within their configured limits.

Σ_j cargoVolume_j,s ≤ usableCargoVolume_s   and   Σ_j onboardMass_j,s ≤ payloadLimit_s
