From simulation to implementation Comparison → Partnerships → Pilot decision

Before adding vehicles, improve how the fleet operates.

Can demand-led parking and shared night-time servicing improve service availability and business contribution with the same fleet? Use the simulator to examine the trade-offs, then define the partnerships and evidence needed to test the concept.

01 / COMPARE

Test the operating model

Hold potential demand and fleet size constant. Compare empty cruising, consolidated night-time inspection and cleaning, nearby private parking, and both measures together.

Trace the economics: completed bookings and pickup waits → cruising distance and road use → energy consumption and storage CAPEX → daily contribution after parking and service costs.

Challenge the assumptions: double the hourly parking rate; reduce the fleet; change available parking spaces. Rerun each case and examine where the benefit disappears.

02 / STRUCTURE PARTNERSHIPS

Build an executable agreement

Fleet operator: dispatch, vehicle availability, service standards and operating data. Parking owners: locations, time windows, access and fees.

Inspection and cleaning partners: task scope, staffing, throughput and completion records. Municipality and local services: local demand, community coordination and suitable pickup locations.

Business development role: bring the parties together, negotiate responsibilities and commercial terms, and connect operating requirements to system specifications and acceptance criteria.

03 / VALIDATE

Decide whether to scale

Begin in one defined service area with a limited fleet and parking network. Establish a measured baseline, then compare matched operating periods while recording demand, fleet availability and service conditions.

Measure: empty kilometres, pickup waits, completed bookings, energy use, parking costs and nightly service completion. Estimate storage requirements from measured energy profiles.

Decision gate: agree targets before the pilot. Expand only if service quality, net contribution and nightly work capacity meet those targets; otherwise revise pricing, locations, staffing or fleet allocation and retest.

A working discussion: start with a regional demand assumption, change one business constraint together, and identify what must be negotiated or measured next.

Independent concept using synthetic regions and editable assumptions, not operating results or confirmed partnerships. Road-use indicators do not predict congestion reduction. Storage CAPEX is a planning estimate; actual sizing requires measured energy profiles and engineering validation. Partnership and pilot steps are a proposed execution plan.

Application prototype

Seneschal

Seneschal combines the one-stop convenience of MaaS with Activity-based Mobility, which organizes transportation around activities. It fills functional gaps that API integration alone cannot address, enabling users to compare activities, services, and transportation as complete options. It provides a consistent experience across delegating arrangements to AI, making choices and adjustments themselves, and checking the status of their services and journeys.

Revision log Rev. 1 · updated 22 Oct 2026

A scheduling app rebuilt for real life: it reads live public data, turns it into action, and is re-checked against production every morning.

  • Real-world signals, built in. Warnings, forecasts, earthquakes, tsunami, typhoons and AMeDAS observations from the Japan Meteorological Agency, plus heat index, elevation and solar radiation, all from free public data.
  • From information to action. Weather lands on the calendar, disasters on a yellow alert card, and parking and charging in the schedule. Bookings go out with congestion-aware travel time.
  • An AI planner you can trust with your calendar. One request becomes schedule options, and nothing is written until you approve. Fixed events never move, a stale proposal cannot be approved, ending the conversation discards unapproved proposals while keeping approved events, and a lost response after saving cannot cause a double write. Behaviour is checked by an independent evaluation ledger of 134 cases (129 pass, 0 fail, 5 inconclusive) and a use-case ledger of 31 request scenarios (13 implemented, 12 waiting on external data), using fixed test data rather than the published app or real calendars.
  • From an earthquake alert to meeting up (demonstration). The alert card offers “consider going home or meeting” and opens a proposal with a meeting candidate on the map. Roads of unknown condition are dashed, a reported closure blocks selection, and nothing is saved before the normal approval step. It runs on demonstration data kept apart from real events.
  • Wider coverage: power and traffic. Electricity supply–demand now covers all nine grid areas (up from one) and the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism traffic API is added. Where no station is near, the search widens from about 3 km to 8 km, and the yellow card flags power-usage rate and severe heat index.
  • Two-way calendar sync, no login. A public sample Google Calendar syncs both ways through change notifications with a retry queue, so nothing is lost. Square results also join the search suggestions, read-only.
  • Ask privately, stay organised. Consultations with the assistant are kept as private conversations in the leftmost Friends tab, grouped into projects, with example prompts alongside. Earlier Seneschal routes redirect there.
  • Every booking checked every morning. Every write operation, including bookings, is tested on each pull request and again each morning against the live app in a real Chrome.
  • Quick from the first screen. The assistant, Federation console, wallet QR, side menu and the 250 KB English dictionary load only when needed.
  • Lean and hardened. 280+ unused components, logic and packages were removed under behaviour-parity tests and before/after screen comparison. Backend functions no longer expose secrets, map overlay HTML is escaped, and the blank clinic screen is now a step-by-step intake.

Prototype with demonstration data where noted in the app. External-data features use free public APIs.

Application prototype

Federation

Federation concretizes the “Industrial Revolution through AI utilization” as an operational foundation in which multiple AIs and systems work collaboratively. It is an integrated platform designed to comprehensively understand interdependencies and supply-demand and resource constraints that cannot be fully captured through individual automation, and to handle everything from detecting operational failures and their early signs to analyzing causes, coordinating responses, and restoring operations.

Revision log Rev. 1 · updated 22 Oct 2026

Federation now proves its own numbers: one source of truth, a profitability gate, and cards that are scored and independently reviewed.

  • One source of truth. The simulator's project-finance engine is the reference for calculated results. Indices stay in Federation and flow out in real time, and Seneschal's numeric baseline follows both.
  • Full project finance, fed back. A 50-line P/L, 2025–2035 cash flow, IRR, NPV and sensitivity are calculated and fed back into the portfolio.
  • A profit is shown only when it holds up. A 2035 profit appears only after full-system verification: revenue must reconcile with operating scale, and depreciation must be explainable from total investment and useful life. With depreciation rebased on the investment amount, 2035 operating profit in the plan scenario is +13.378 B JPY.
  • A profit search that only proposes. The search for the most profitable conditions runs in the simulator and suggests changes without overwriting the plan.
  • Fresh outside information, approved by a person. Outside data is gathered into dated proposals to update indices (latest 19 Oct), and a person approves each one.
  • Quality you can check, not just claim. Blueprint and Artifact cards are scored continuously on a 10-point rubric, and only the independent review of a card's current content counts. Diagnosis, correction, evidence and re-review are traced in one workspace, and reviewers themselves are measured by planting known errors. With 97 cards in the ledger, each carrying its score and open findings, quality is something you can check rather than a claim.
  • Documents and numbers kept in step. An automatic check against the English portfolio keeps the Blueprint cards consistent with it, and the cards were expanded to match. Two original-application images were transcribed and checked against the canonical numbers.
  • From detection to verification in one flow. Seven Runtime tabs each have their own screen and follow one operating flow: coordination and detection, cause analysis, candidate comparison, verification. Every result says whether it comes from an execution record or an estimate from current inputs. A hand-over list runs through every tab and can be shared through Base44, so each person in charge sees the same inbox.
  • From specification to decisions. The Design section renders the v2 specification as a model: state machines, sequence, use-case and activity diagrams, a requirement-to-evidence trace, consistency checks and upstream-and-downstream change-impact analysis. Gaps and open questions are listed, and a desk check is never shown as field-verified. It also lets you work with the specification: a 2035 vehicle model ties driving, fuel cell, storage, refuelling and heat to bill-of-materials mass, and a decision board shows, for each design decision, what every option changes, a recommended option and a due date.
  • Notes you can navigate. The note vault is an Obsidian-style graph with hover preview, a neighbours-only view, and keyboard and pinch-zoom access. Notes with content were imported, and sourced service-operations notes for autonomous mobility are linked in.
