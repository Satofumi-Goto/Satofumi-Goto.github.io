Autonomous mobility, designed for viable operations.

I develop new mobility businesses by connecting vehicles with cities—their services, hubs, and infrastructure—and bringing together the expertise and partnerships needed to turn these connections into customer value and viable operations.

Profile

My strength is combining technical planning, business development, and B2B sales to turn customer needs and technical and operational conditions into executable business plans.

I joined DENSO in 1997 and built my experience in automotive B2B sales. Since 2013, I have worked in business development across the CASE and MaaS domains. At Honda, my primary responsibilities include evaluating business viability and product requirements for external PEFC commercialization. Beyond this scope, I have brought forward broader mobility business and product proposals, contributing to management-level decisions and cross-functional progress in business and product development.

I work with customers, companies, and municipalities to establish shared challenges and objectives, translating expertise from each field into business plans and system requirements. By understanding the interdependencies among demand, operations, vehicles, and energy, I align customer value, operating conditions, and business viability to connect concepts with implementation.

My goal is to establish sustainable mobility businesses that connect the movement of people and goods with everyday activities and local services. This site presents independent studies that put this approach into concrete form through business models, system designs, a simulator, and application prototypes.

Before adding vehicles, improve how the fleet operates.

Can demand-led parking and shared night-time servicing improve service availability and business contribution with the same fleet? Use the simulator to examine the trade-offs, then define the partnerships and evidence needed to test the concept.

01 / COMPARE

Test the operating model

Hold potential demand and fleet size constant. Compare empty cruising, consolidated night-time inspection and cleaning, nearby private parking, and both measures together.

Trace the economics: completed bookings and pickup waits → cruising distance and road use → energy consumption and storage CAPEX → daily contribution after parking and service costs.

Challenge the assumptions: double the hourly parking rate; reduce the fleet; change available parking spaces. Rerun each case and examine where the benefit disappears.

02 / STRUCTURE PARTNERSHIPS

Build an executable agreement

Fleet operator: dispatch, vehicle availability, service standards and operating data. Parking owners: locations, time windows, access and fees.

Inspection and cleaning partners: task scope, staffing, throughput and completion records. Municipality and local services: local demand, community coordination and suitable pickup locations.

Business development role: bring the parties together, negotiate responsibilities and commercial terms, and connect operating requirements to system specifications and acceptance criteria.

03 / VALIDATE

Decide whether to scale

Begin in one defined service area with a limited fleet and parking network. Establish a measured baseline, then compare matched operating periods while recording demand, fleet availability and service conditions.

Measure: empty kilometres, pickup waits, completed bookings, energy use, parking costs and nightly service completion. Estimate storage requirements from measured energy profiles.

Decision gate: agree targets before the pilot. Expand only if service quality, net contribution and nightly work capacity meet those targets; otherwise revise pricing, locations, staffing or fleet allocation and retest.

A working discussion: start with a regional demand assumption, change one business constraint together, and identify what must be negotiated or measured next.

Independent concept using synthetic regions and editable assumptions, not operating results or confirmed partnerships. Road-use indicators do not predict congestion reduction. Storage CAPEX is a planning estimate; actual sizing requires measured energy profiles and engineering validation. Partnership and pilot steps are a proposed execution plan.

It fills functional gaps that API integration alone cannot address, enabling users to compare activities, services, and transportation as complete options. It is an integrated platform designed to comprehensively understand interdependencies and supply-demand and resource constraints that cannot be fully captured through individual automation, and to handle everything from detecting operational failures and their early signs to analyzing causes, coordinating responses, and restoring operations. About this work

These portfolios and prototypes are independent studies based on public information and original analysis. They explore the conditions for launching and sustaining autonomous mobility services.

Professional experience includes proposals, technical analysis and cross-functional coordination that contributed to management-level and product-development decisions at Honda. The public-facing diagrams, architectures, simulations and prototypes presented here were separately prepared using public information and independently defined assumptions; they are not reproductions of Honda's internal documents, confidential data, proprietary engineering files or source code.

The FC skateboard and integrated FCAD-MaaS vehicle, operations-platform and service-hub concepts illustrate business and product ideas I examined and proposed. The specific designs, assumptions and scenarios published here are not presented as Honda-issued specifications, formally approved project plans or demonstrated deployment results.
